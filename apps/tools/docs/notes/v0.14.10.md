### Fixes
- **DoH now carries the browser whole.** Until now only requests the app could make for a page (GETs on names the network's DNS hid) went through the resolver; the page's own connections — and so most of its loading, every form sent, every site check — still went the network's way, which is why a blocked site stayed blocked. Now, while DoH is on, **the app runs a small proxy of its own on the phone (127.0.0.1) and points its web views at it**: every connection a page makes comes through it. There the site's name is asked of the resolver, never the network's DNS; an HTTPS connection is tunnelled as it is (TLS stays between the page and the site, so certificates, cookies and a site's check are the browser's own) with **its first record — the TLS hello — cut in two on the wire** for a site the network hides or blocks, so a filter reading the site's name from the first packet reads none; plain HTTP is forwarded by the address found. Forms, logins, WebSockets and the app's own sources go through it too.
- A site whose connection dies before the site said anything, or shows a certificate that is not its own (a filter's page in its place), is marked and goes through the split hello from then on; the browser loads the page again by itself.
- The resolvers' JSON form is asked only of those that answer it (Quad9 and ControlD do not); error messages are shown clean.
