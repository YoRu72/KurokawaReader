### Fixes
- **The settings backup no longer carries your sign-in keys.** Settings → Backup saved every setting into the file, your AniList sign-in and your debrid key with them — a file people pass around. It now leaves out every key, token and password, as the other backup always did. Make a new backup if you shared an older one.
- **Unsaved edits are no longer lost when you close.** The subtitle workshop and the text editor in Files ask before closing with changes not saved — by the close button, Back, Escape or a swipe: *Save*, *Discard* or *Keep editing*. Saving over a file that is there asks first.
- **Emptying the bin asks first**, with how many items: the Files bin and the removed downloads. Both were emptied for good at one tap.
- **Locking files in the vault keeps the originals unless you say otherwise.** The «delete the originals» box was ticked by default; it is now unticked, with a word on why: leave it so until you have opened a locked copy with your password.
- **Unmarking an episode no longer changes AniList at once.** It set the progress to the episode before, with no way back. The new progress shows at once with *Undo*; AniList is told only when the Undo is gone, so an Undo leaves your list on AniList exactly as it was.
- **Restoring a backup says what it will change** (how many settings, how many differ from yours) and asks first. After the reload, *Undo restore* puts your own values back.
- Renaming files back with Undo stops, and says so, if one of them was changed in between: nothing is renamed then.

It installs for you only, in a few seconds, with no administrator prompt. Also the classic installer and a portable zip.

### Moving from the previous build
- **Update from inside the app, as always.** The previous build gets your data ready first — settings, your anime, TV and video lists, watch progress and subtitle choices, add-ons, history, sign-ins, your icon —, pauses your downloads cleanly and notes which were running, writes it all to a folder beside your data, checks every file, and only then installs the new app. If anything fails, nothing is installed, your downloads go on, and the app says why.
- **Your downloads go on where they stopped**, from the same folders: the ones that were running start again by themselves, the ones you had paused stay paused. Nothing is downloaded twice, no file is moved.
- **On its first start the new app offers to bring the rest over** (*Bring it now*). It is copied, never moved: **nothing is deleted**, and you can free the space of the copy afterwards when you choose.
- **You can say *Not now*.** The move is offered again a day later (a week if you check for updates weekly), or at once for a newer version.
- Installed the new app by hand while the previous one is still there? The previous one is kept beside it — with your downloads, and still opening your torrents and magnet links — until it has carried your data over: open it once and let it update.
- **Portable copy and macOS:** your data is made ready and your downloads are paused; open the new copy (the new portable zip, or the new app dragged to Applications) and it brings everything over.
- **What comes along:** sign-ins inside the app's browser that kept you signed in between visits (their cookies travel in a file only you can read, deleted as soon as the new app has taken them); AniList and your debrid service stay signed in; yt-dlp, gallery-dl and the ffmpeg pack when they are exactly the official files (checked against the official checksums) — any other copy is downloaded again the first time it is needed.
- **The previous build stays on the release page for two versions** (`*-win-x64.exe`, `*-mac-universal.dmg`, `*-linux-x86_64.AppImage`) for anyone who needs it. If the new macOS build is missing from a release, the previous build stays the Mac download and keeps updating as before.

### What is still different in the new app
- **Sign-in windows ask once more** after the move; a sign-in that lasted only one visit in the app's browser asks again too. A site that signs you in for all its subdomains may ask again on a subdomain.
- **Clicking a finished download's notice opens it** on Windows (the installed app) and on Linux. On macOS and in the Windows portable copy, bringing the app to the front within two minutes of the notice opens it instead.
- **Grab in the app's browser** finds videos and music a site gives no telling name by asking the site what it served — one quick question for each address the page itself loaded, never to another site, a few at a time. It can add a few seconds, and an address that has already expired is missed.
- **Files are no longer renamed after download** when their name says nothing about them.
- **DNS over HTTPS** covers the app's own requests; pages shown inside it use the system's DNS.
- **The Wi-Fi share** shows the address of the connection your computer goes out through.
- **The screen translator:** a game or video in exclusive full screen may stay above it (borderless or windowed full screen is fine); on macOS the caption has its own dark box; on Linux under Wayland the caption goes where the desktop puts it, each capture may ask the desktop's permission, and the hotkeys work only while an X11 program has the keyboard.
- **The window** opens at its usual size the first time.
- **YouTube in the video tools** needs deno or node for some formats. With neither installed, the app offers *Get deno*: a small download, checked against deno's own release.

### Themes
- **Frosted glass, calm and easy on the eyes.** Soft glass panes over near-neutral grounds — a soft charcoal in the dark, a quiet paper in the light. The themes: **Frost × Material 3 Expressive** (the new default), **Aurora**, **Obsidian** (true black for OLED screens), **Indigo Paper**, and **Frosted Glass**, **Frosted Glass Light** and **Frosted Glass Dark** with **pictures to download** (shared under the MIT licence; Settings → About lists it). Colours you chose before are kept inside the glass. **Classic** — the look you had — is one tap away in Settings → Appearance. Glass blur: Automatic, Full, Reduced or Off — Automatic lightens it on low battery.
- **The theme editor and My themes.** Customise any theme with a live preview on the app itself — the glass, the tint, the blur, the shape and depth, the background (the theme's own, soft colour fields, one colour, a downloaded picture or your own) —, with Undo and Redo and a warning when text would be hard to read. Save it under a name; edit it again, duplicate, rename, delete (with Undo) or pass it on as a file.

### Undo and Redo
- **Undo where it was missing:** a download removed from the list (Redo removes it again), a reset of the player's sound, picture or toolbar, the subtitle delay, a bookmark, a follow, a preset, a rule, a font, a season marked as seen, a theme deleted. On a computer: **Ctrl+Z** and **Ctrl+Shift+Z** (or Ctrl+Y), never while you type in a box.
- **The subtitle workshop** takes every edit back one step at a time (timing shifts and stretches too), and its layout is redone: Timing, Translation and Dubbing.

### Android
- **The download engine in Rust, to try** (Settings → Advanced → Download engine). Java stays the default; Rust is new — try it on Wi-Fi and on mobile data, and switch back if anything goes wrong. When the Rust engine meets a problem, Java takes the download over. Torrents and videos are not affected.
