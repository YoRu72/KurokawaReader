### Fixes
- **No more crash while torrents run or are removed.** The app could close by itself (a native crash in the torrent engine) when the download list was read while a torrent was being removed, or while a stream was playing from it. The app now keeps its own copy of each torrent's file list, so nothing is read after the engine lets go of it.
- **Download notifications now show by default.** Android 13 and later hide an app's notifications until it asks; the app now asks when you add your first download. The notification shows the progress, the size done of the total, the speed and the time left; with several downloads, each gets its own line. *Settings → Download notifications* says whether they are allowed and opens Android's page if they were turned off.
- **Resuming no longer starts again from zero:**
  - On sites whose file tag changes between visits (CDNs, signed links), resuming used to throw the download away. Now the bytes already downloaded are compared with the server's (the start and end of several pieces), and the download goes on from where it stopped when they match.
  - When the progress record is lost but the partial file is still there, the pieces already in it are found, checked against the server, and kept.
  - A piece stopped halfway keeps what it had (only whole pieces were kept before).
  - Pieces that were never really written (the phone stopped before saving them) are fetched again instead of leaving a hole.
- **The APK installer needs much less free space.** To show what an app is, it no longer copies the whole base APK out of a bundle; it keeps only the manifest, the resources and the signature (a few MB at most), as HatchZip does. Installing still streams each part straight into Android's installer, now with bigger buffers, so it is faster.

### New
- **Translate on screen** — a new add-on (Settings → Add-ons):
  - **On Android, a floating bubble over every app.**
    - Tap it to translate what the screen shows, in place, over the words.
    - It reads the screen's text exactly (turn on its accessibility service), or a picture of the screen read by OCR, for games, comics and videos.
    - Draw a region to translate, or keep a fixed region for a game's dialogue box or a video's subtitles.
    - **Auto translation:** the translations follow the screen as you scroll, or every few seconds.
    - The translations can sit on the screen's own colours, or appear as a list you can copy.
    - Choose what a tap, a double tap and a long press do. The bubble fades when idle, keeps to the edge, and closes when you drop it on ✕.
    - Also: reading aloud, a history, and a quick-settings tile to bring it back.
  - **On a computer, shortcuts that work in any app.**
    - Draw a region on the frozen screen and its text is translated over it; or translate the whole screen.
    - Watch the last region (subtitles, dialogue) and see its translation in a small window beneath it.
    - Translate the clipboard by the mouse.
    - It can stay in the tray and start with the computer.
    - Text is read by the computer's own OCR (Windows OCR, macOS Vision, or Tesseract on Linux), or by your AI model.
  - **Translators:** Google Translate (free, no key), DeepL (your key), or your AI model as in Kurokawa Reader (Gemini, OpenAI, Claude, OpenRouter or your own API), with your own instructions.
- **The player translates subtitles.** In the subtitle sheet, *Translate the subtitles* makes a translated track with the same translator, starting from the lines where the video is. The original can stay on a second line above it. You can also choose any other track as the second line.
