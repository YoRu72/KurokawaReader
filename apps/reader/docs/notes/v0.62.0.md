### Fixes
- **A PDF no longer loses its page after the window is resized.** After a resize or going full screen, the page counter and the pages drawn could drift a few pages away from the page on the screen. They now follow the page you are on.
- **Your reading place is kept when the window changes size.** Resizing, going full screen, changing the interface size or turning the screen no longer moves the text away from the paragraph you were reading. Scroll yourself while it happens and your own place is kept instead.
- **A chapter turned by a swipe or a key stays turned** even if the screen turns or the window resizes right after.
- **Your own icon shows everywhere in the app**, including the library header in a narrow window and the update sheet.

### Computer: a new, far smaller app
- **Windows: 12.9 MB instead of 81.6 MB.** A new setup program with one screen: *Install*, and *Options* for the folder, a desktop shortcut, opening EPUB, PDF and other books with KurokawaReader, and opening it when done. It installs for you only, in a few seconds, with no administrator prompt, shows real progress and says plainly what went wrong if something does. Also a portable zip (10 MB instead of 81.5 MB) and the classic installer (9.5 MB).
- **macOS: about 10 MB instead of 166 MB. Linux: an 11 MB .deb**, or the AppImage.
- **Starts faster**: about half a second to the library on Windows once installed, and no white flash — the window opens in your theme's colour.
- **Its own title bar**, drawn in the app's colours, with settings to keep it solid or see-through over the book, or to use the system's frame instead.
- **Your own app icon** on the window, the taskbar, the shortcuts and everywhere inside the app.
- **Archives** open, extract and are made as before, with the 7-Zip program that comes with the app. **Notifications** when a conversion or a download ends in the background come from the system, as before.
- **The PDF → Word converter uses less memory.**
- **Updates** are downloaded, checked against the release before anything is installed, resumed if the connection breaks, and installed in place.

### What the update does to your library
- **Update from inside the app** (*Settings → App updates*). The update first prepares your library — books, notes, highlights, reading places, folders, tags, settings, sign-ins, your icon — writes it to a folder beside your data, checks every file, and only then installs the new app. If anything fails, nothing is installed, the app keeps working and tells you why.
- On its first start the new app offers to bring it all over (*Bring it now*). It is copied, never moved: **nothing is deleted from your library**, and you can free the space of the copy afterwards when you choose.
- If the free space on the disk cannot be read, the update tells you how much it needs and asks before going on.
- **You can say *Not now*.** The move is offered again at the next update check, not at every start.
- Installed the new app by hand while the previous one is still there? The previous one is kept beside it until it has carried your library over: open it once and let it update. The new app's first screen says so, and can also restore a backup file.

### What is different
- **Print to PDF** in the converter is replaced by the picture PDF (pages as pictures with searchable text over them), as on the web version.
- **macOS**: your custom icon is not put on the Dock icon; the system does not let the app change it there. Drag the new app to Applications when its disk image opens.
- **Sign-ins inside the in-app browser** (sites opened from the sources) are asked again once. Sync and AniList stay signed in.
- **Linux AppImage**: about the same size as before (it carries its own web engine); the .deb is the small one.
- **The previous build stays on the release page for two versions** (`…-win-x64.exe`, `…-mac-universal.dmg`, `…-linux-x86_64.AppImage`) for anyone who needs it. If the new macOS build is missing from a release, the previous build remains the Mac download and keeps updating as before.
